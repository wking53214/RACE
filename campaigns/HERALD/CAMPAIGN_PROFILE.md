# RACE CAMPAIGN PROFILE — HERALD

## 1. Campaign Identity

- Campaign: HERALD
- RACE phase structure: PHASE_0, PHASE_1, PHASE_2
- Campaign status: PLANNED
- Target: HERALD repository and its relevant integrations
- Profile version: 1.0

## 2. Campaign Mission

Conduct a source-level, evidence-driven investigation of HERALD.

Establish what the repository demonstrably does, how its components operate, what capabilities are incomplete or unverified, what dependencies and integration relationships exist, and what risks or architectural weaknesses require attention.

The campaign must distinguish implemented behavior from intended behavior and documented claims.

## 3. Mission Objectives

- Establish repository identity, structure, and purpose.
- Inspect relevant source code, configuration, tests, and documentation.
- Identify actual execution paths and component responsibilities.
- Assess security, governance, authorization, and trust boundaries.
- Identify verified and proposed dependencies and integrations.
- Examine failure handling and operational limitations.
- Conduct adversarial analysis of material claims and assumptions.
- Produce evidence-supported findings and recommendations.
- Establish whether proposed changes are justified.
- Define objective acceptance criteria for any authorized implementation.

## 4. Permanent Expert Panel

Every campaign phase must include all three permanent seats:

- UCO-001 — Universal Chief Officer.
- HACKER-001 — The Hacker.
- MACG-001 — MacG.

These three seats are mandatory and cannot be replaced or removed. Additional mission-specific experts may be introduced when justified by the subject matter. They supplement the permanent panel.

### 4.2 Mission Assignments and Capability Selection

For each expert, record the mission assignment, expected contribution, limitations, specialist dependencies, and evidence of actual participation.

For every selected capability, regardless of expert ownership, record:

1. Capability ID.
2. Capability name.
3. Applicable mission question.
4. Expected analytical contribution.
5. Relevant evidence requirements.
6. Known limitations.
7. Specialist dependency.

Do not select or invent formal capability IDs for an expert whose capability library is not formalized.

Capability selection does not prove that the capability was exercised, demonstrated, or successful. Record actual participation and supporting evidence separately.

A simulated expert perspective within one AI execution is not an independently executed agent. Do not claim separate execution without evidence.

Preserve the exact expert configuration in the mission snapshot and generated prompt record. Mission assignments do not silently change expert identities.

## 5. Scope and Constraints

The exact repository scope, branch, commit, relevant integrations, and exclusions must be established during Phase 0.

Do not assume that documentation accurately describes runtime behavior.

Do not assume that a dependency or integration is operational merely because it is mentioned in a README, configuration file, or design document.

Do not modify source code during discovery.

Do not commit, push, deploy, publish, or perform external actions without explicit human authorization.

## 6. Evidence Requirements

Use the RACE evidence classifications:

- EXECUTED
- INSPECTED
- INFERRED
- UNKNOWN

Material findings must reference supporting evidence.

For software investigations, prioritize:

1. Reproducible runtime behavior.
2. Reproducible test results.
3. Inspected source code and configuration.
4. Build and execution records.
5. Documentation.
6. Unverified statements.

Record contradictions, unavailable evidence, and inspection limitations.

## 7. Adversarial Requirements

Challenge:

- Architectural assumptions.
- Claims of completed functionality.
- Security and authorization boundaries.
- Dependency and integration claims.
- Failure handling.
- Test coverage and test validity.
- Operational readiness.
- Evidence quality.

Identify both confirmed weaknesses and plausible but unverified risks.

Do not represent an untested attack path as a successful exploit.

## 8. Phase Boundaries

### Phase 0 — Discovery and Blueprint

Investigate, challenge, document findings, and propose an architecture or improvement plan.

No implementation authority is implied.

### Phase 1 — Controlled Construction

Perform only work explicitly authorized against the accepted Phase 0 baseline.

Record every material change and its authorization.

### Phase 2 — Verification and Closure

Independently verify requirements, implementation claims, and adversarial controls.

Document failures, residual risks, and acceptance decisions.

Completion of one phase does not automatically authorize the next.

## 9. Required Deliverables

- Phase 0 investigation and architectural blueprint.
- Phase 0 manifest.
- Phase 1 implementation record and manifest, if authorized.
- Phase 2 verification report and manifest, if authorized.
- Repository profile.
- Evidence register.
- Findings and recommendation register.
- Outstanding issues and risk register.
- Final campaign handoff and closure assessment.

## 10. Recommendation Vocabulary

Every material recommendation must use one of:

- KEEP
- INTEGRATE
- ADAPT
- PARTIAL
- SEPARATE
- DEFER
- REJECT
- NO ACTION

Recommendations require supporting evidence and rationale.

## 11. Campaign Completion

HERALD is not complete merely because the three phases have been attempted.

Completion requires:

- Required deliverables accounted for.
- Evidence traceability.
- Documented acceptance assessment.
- Outstanding findings and risks dispositioned.
- Human closure decision.
- Explicit identification of work not performed.

## 12. Governing Rule

HERALD is the first application of RACE, not an exception to RACE.

The campaign must follow the same evidence, authorization, adversarial, and handoff requirements that RACE establishes for every future mission.
