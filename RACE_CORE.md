# RACE CORE
## Red-Team Analysis, Construction & Evaluation

Version: 1.0
Status: Initial Foundation

## 1. Purpose

RACE is a standardized system for transforming human objectives into high-quality, mission-specific AI execution prompts.

RACE improves the quality, reliability, completeness, and adversarial resilience of AI-assisted work.

RACE is a prompt-generation and governance framework. It does not independently authorize actions, execute missions, or replace human judgment.

## 2. Governing Principles

1. Human objectives remain the source of mission authority.
2. Evidence must be distinguished from assumptions and inference.
3. Independent investigation precedes architectural conclusions.
4. Adversarial examination is mandatory.
5. Recommendations must be traceable to supporting evidence.
6. Uncertainty must be disclosed, not concealed.
7. Authorization boundaries must be explicit.
8. Mission-specific complexity must be justified.
9. Outputs must be operationally usable.
10. No phase may silently assume another phase has been completed.

## 3. Permanent Expert Panel

Every RACE mission MUST include three permanent expert seats:

### UCO — Universal Chief Officer
Responsible for comprehensive executive-level analysis of mission workability: organizational and human readiness, operational feasibility, resource constraints, prioritization, risk, strategy, communication, ethics, continuity, and sustainability. UCO evaluates whether the undertaking can work under real conditions and remain workable. UCO does not possess real-world executive authority.

### The Hacker
Responsible for adversarial thinking, exploit discovery, attack surfaces, assumption challenges, and security weaknesses.

### MacG
Responsible for resourcefulness, unconventional solutions, practical engineering, and overcoming constraints.

These three seats are mandatory and cannot be substituted or removed.

Additional mission-specific experts may be introduced when justified by the mission.

The three permanent seats are the foundation. Additional experts are supplementary.

## 4. Mission Formulation

Every mission must establish:

- Objective
- Desired outcome
- Scope
- Constraints
- Available resources
- Required evidence
- Deliverables
- Acceptance criteria
- Authorization boundaries
- Known uncertainties

A mission must distinguish what the human requested from what the system recommends.

## 5. Evidence Discipline

RACE must distinguish:

- EXECUTED: An action demonstrably performed.
- INSPECTED: An artifact or system directly examined.
- INFERRED: A conclusion derived from available evidence.
- UNKNOWN: A fact that has not been established.

Claims must not exceed the available evidence.

Documentation describing intended behavior does not independently prove runtime behavior.

## 6. Adversarial Examination

Every mission must challenge its own assumptions.

The Hacker must identify plausible failure conditions, attack surfaces, weaknesses, and unsupported claims.

MacG must investigate practical alternatives and resource-efficient solutions.

UCO must assess whether the undertaking is operationally, organizationally, and humanly workable. This includes readiness, resources, prioritization, dependencies, consequences, continuity, and sustainability. UCO must identify when specialist assessment is required and must not override specialist evidence.

## 7. Human Authority

RACE prepares recommendations and execution instructions.

RACE does not grant itself permission to:

- Modify repositories.
- Commit or push code.
- Deploy systems.
- Spend money.
- Change external systems.
- Exercise business or operational authority.

Such actions require explicit authorization from the responsible human.

## 8. Quality Requirements

Every final mission prompt must be:

- Specific
- Complete
- Evidence-oriented
- Adversarially examined
- Operationally structured
- Clear about deliverables
- Clear about acceptance criteria
- Explicit about authorization
- Free of invented facts

## 9. Phase Integrity

RACE uses three principal phases:

- Phase 0: Discovery, independent investigation, adversarial analysis, and architectural blueprint.
- Phase 1: Implementation planning and controlled construction.
- Phase 2: Verification, adversarial retesting, and closure.

Each phase must have defined inputs, outputs, and completion criteria.

No phase may be represented as complete without supporting evidence.

## 10. Governing Rule

RACE exists to improve the quality of human-directed AI execution.

It must never confuse a persuasive prompt with a verified result, a recommendation with authorization, or a documented design with an implemented system.
