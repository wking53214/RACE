# RACE EXPERT DEVELOPMENT LOG

## 1. Purpose

This log preserves the development history of RACE expert identities,
capabilities, and operating doctrines.

It provides provenance, accountability, and historical reconstruction.

## 2. Required Development Entry

Every proposed or approved change must record:

- Development entry ID.
- Expert ID.
- Affected component: identity, capability, or doctrine.
- Existing version.
- Proposed version.
- Date and time.
- Originating mission or research activity.
- Description of the proposed change.
- Evidence references and provenance.
- Reason the change is relevant.
- Overlap and compatibility assessment.
- Potential unintended consequences.
- Evaluation performed.
- Evaluation result.
- Human approval decision.
- Publication status.
- Validation status.

Unknown information must be recorded as UNKNOWN rather than invented.

## 3. Development Lifecycle

Use this progression:

OBSERVED
A relevant finding is supported by cited evidence or direct inspection.

PROPOSED
A specific improvement has been described.

UNDER_REVIEW
Evidence, relevance, compatibility, and risks are being evaluated.

APPROVED
A human explicitly authorizes the change.

IMPLEMENTED
The approved change has been incorporated into a versioned artifact.

VALIDATED
The implemented change has passed defined validation criteria.

REJECTED
The proposal was reviewed and rejected, with reasons preserved.

DEFERRED
The proposal is retained for possible future consideration.

These labels describe development states, not proof of capability.

## 4. Research and Continuous Development

Research may identify new methods, standards, techniques, tools,
vulnerabilities, or domain knowledge relevant to an expert.

Research findings must be assessed for:
- Source credibility.
- Recency.
- Relevance.
- Applicability.
- Conflicting evidence.
- Limitations.
- Potential effects on existing capabilities.

Research may generate proposals. It must not automatically alter permanent
expert identities or approved capability libraries.

## 5. Identity Protection

Identity revisions require stricter review than ordinary capability
additions.

A capability improvement must not silently alter the expert's defining
perspective, behavioral boundaries, or relationship with the permanent panel.

## 6. Mission Learning

A mission may produce findings that are useful to a permanent expert.

Those findings must first be evaluated as potential improvements.

Mission-specific knowledge does not automatically become permanent expert
knowledge.

## 7. Historical Preservation

Never overwrite historical development entries.

Substantive changes require new version records.

Preserve rejected, deferred, retired, and superseded decisions so future
development can understand what was considered and why.

## 8. Human Approval

No expert, receiving AI, or automated process may independently approve
its own permanent identity or capability changes.

Human approval must be explicit and documented.
