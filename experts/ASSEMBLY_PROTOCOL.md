# RACE EXPERT ASSEMBLY PROTOCOL

## 1. Purpose

This protocol governs how permanent and mission-specific experts are
represented in RACE prompts.

Its purpose is to ensure consistent expert identity, relevant capability
selection, functional panel participation, and historical traceability.

## 2. Mandatory Permanent Panel

Every RACE mission must include:

1. UCO — Universal Chief Officer.
2. The Hacker.
3. MacG.

No mission may silently remove, replace, or omit these seats.

Mission-specific experts may supplement the permanent panel when justified.

## 3. Assembly Components

An assembled expert contribution must distinguish:

IDENTITY:
The canonical expert identity and its approved version.

CAPABILITIES:
The approved capability modules relevant to the mission.

OPERATING DOCTRINE:
The rules governing how the expert applies its capabilities and reports.

MISSION ASSIGNMENT:
Temporary objectives, context, constraints, and questions for that mission.

These components must not be conflated.

## 4. Capability Selection

The mission determines which approved capabilities receive emphasis.

Selection must be based on mission relevance.

The assembly process must not:
- Invent capabilities.
- Present proposed capabilities as approved.
- Add irrelevant material merely to make the prompt longer.
- Alter permanent identity to fit a mission.
- Omit a mandatory permanent expert.
- Claim that an expert has performed an investigation that has not occurred.

Capability approval and performance validation are separate statuses.

An approved capability may be selected for a mission even when its
performance has not been validated. The assembly must disclose both
statuses accurately.

Selection does not establish that a capability was exercised, demonstrated,
or successful.

When a capability is relevant but not approved, not established, or not
validated, its status and limitations must remain visible. Proposed
capabilities must not be presented as approved.

## 5. Functional Participation

Every permanent expert must have a substantive analytical assignment.

A name in a panel roster is not sufficient.

The prompt must establish:
- What the expert is expected to examine.
- What distinctive perspective it contributes.
- What it must challenge.
- What boundaries it must respect.
- What contribution it must return.

The depth of participation must be proportional to the mission.

Experts must not manufacture irrelevant findings to demonstrate activity.

## 6. Panel Independence and Interaction

Permanent experts must retain distinct responsibilities.

They may challenge one another's assumptions and recommendations.

Disagreement must not be silently removed to manufacture consensus.

The receiving AI must preserve material unresolved objections.

A simulated expert perspective within one AI execution must not be described
as an independently executed agent or independent verification.

Claims of independent review require evidence that separate execution
actually occurred.

## 7. Mission Snapshot

Every assembled RACE prompt must preserve enough information to reconstruct
the expert configuration used.

A mission snapshot must record:

- Mission or campaign identifier.
- Assembly date and time, including timezone.
- RACE framework version or baseline.
- Expert IDs and canonical names.
- Identity versions and identity approval status.
- Capability library versions and capability approval status.
- Capability performance-validation status.
- Every selected capability ID and name.
- For each selected capability:
  - Applicable mission question.
  - Expected analytical contribution.
  - Relevant evidence requirements.
  - Known limitations.
  - Specialist dependency.
- Operating doctrine versions and status.
- Mission-specific assignments.
- Additional specialists and justification.
- Known gaps in canonical identity or capability formalization.
- Exact assembled expert content or immutable references.
- Prompt version or prompt hash, when available.
- Known assembly limitations.

A selected capability must not be recorded as exercised merely because it
appears in the assembly. Mission results must separately document actual
exercise and supporting evidence.

For a permanent seat whose canonical identity or approved capability
library has not been formalized, record that status explicitly. Do not
invent a version, capability, approval, or validation status. Its mission
assignment must remain distinguishable from a formally approved permanent
identity or capability definition.

Do not invent hashes or claim immutable storage when it has not been
implemented.

## 8. Version Availability

If a required canonical identity, identity version, or approved
capability version is missing, the assembly must disclose the gap.

A mandatory seat may not be silently omitted or replaced. Where its
canonical identity or capability library is not yet formalized, disclose
that limitation and identify the functional mission assignment separately.

It must not silently substitute a different identity or capability version.

## 9. Authority

Expert assembly does not authorize mission execution or repository changes.

The authorization rules of RACE remain controlling.

## 10. Assembly Acceptance

An assembly is acceptable only when:

- All three permanent seats are present.
- The identity and version status of all three seats is accurately recorded, with missing or unformalized versions explicitly disclosed.
- Their assignments are functionally distinct.
- Selected capabilities are approved or their limitations are disclosed.
- Mission-specific specialists are justified.
- Human authority boundaries are preserved.
- The configuration can be reconstructed from the snapshot.
