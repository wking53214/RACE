# RACE HANDOFF PROTOCOL
Version: 1.0

## 1. Purpose

Establish consistent transfer of mission context, evidence, decisions, deliverables, and outstanding work between RACE phases.

## 2. Handoff Principle

A phase handoff must preserve enough information for the next phase to proceed without reconstructing the previous investigation from incomplete notes.

## 3. Required Handoff Information

Every formal handoff should include:

1. Campaign identifier.
2. Mission objective.
3. Source phase.
4. Destination phase.
5. Deliverables produced.
6. Evidence references.
7. Verified findings.
8. Inferences.
9. Unknowns.
10. Decisions and their rationale.
11. Dependencies.
12. Known risks.
13. Outstanding issues.
14. Required approvals.
15. Destination-phase entry conditions.

## 4. Evidence Continuity

Evidence references must remain identifiable across phases.

A downstream phase must not silently change the meaning or classification of an earlier finding.

If evidence is superseded or contradicted, the change must be documented.

## 5. Decision Continuity

Record material decisions and their dispositions.

Recommended disposition labels:

- KEEP
- INTEGRATE
- ADAPT
- PARTIAL
- SEPARATE
- DEFER
- REJECT
- NO ACTION

Each disposition should include a rationale and supporting evidence where applicable.

## 6. Open Issues

Outstanding questions, unresolved defects, and unverified dependencies must transfer explicitly.

An issue must not disappear merely because a phase has ended.

## 7. Acceptance

The receiving phase must confirm that required deliverables and entry conditions are present.

Incomplete handoffs must be identified and returned for correction or accepted through an explicitly documented human decision.

## 8. Phase Independence

A downstream phase must not treat an earlier phase's recommendation as an approved implementation instruction.

Required authorization must be independently established.

## 9. Closure

Final campaign closure should record:

- Completed objectives.
- Accepted deliverables.
- Verification results.
- Remaining limitations.
- Deferred work.
- Outstanding risks.
- Human acceptance or rejection.
- Final campaign disposition.

## 10. Governing Rule

A handoff transfers information and accountability. It does not transfer or create authority.
